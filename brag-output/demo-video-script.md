# SpeechMate: product presentation + demo video script (FINAL DRAFT)

**Target length:** about 4:45 (fits 4–5 min) · **Style:** product presentation with a live demo
**Narration:** about 720 words, which is roughly 4:45 at 150 words per minute. Read it aloud once with a timer and cut lines if you run over.

Each scene has four parts. **Screen** is what the viewer sees, **Mascot** is which character is on screen and what it does, **Say** is the voiceover, and **Note** is for you only.

| Time | Part |
|---|---|
| 0:00–1:15 | Story → problem → introducing SpeechMate (and Mate) |
| 1:15–3:55 | Demo: profile · **Feature 1** Mock interview (+ results) · **Feature 2** Presentation practice (+ **Q&A rehearsal**) · **Feature 3** Daily conversation |
| 3:55–4:45 | How agentic AI improves learning → close |

---

## The cast

Every character below already exists in the app. Record them from the real UI. In each live session the partner shows **"sleeping"** before you press start and **"working"** while it thinks or speaks.

| Character | Look | Feature / agent it stands for in the video | Where to capture it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mate** | Blob with headphones and big eyes | Your companion · the **learner profile** · the **coach** that picks your next practice | Bottom corner of every page (hidden on the live session page). Click it to open its chat. It hops, spins and dozes off after a minute idle |
| **The interviewer** | Robot with square glasses and a bow tie | **Feature 1, Mock interview** · the **question planner** and **interviewer** agents | Live interview session; the clay **briefcase** scene on `/interview` |
| **The presenter's partner** | Droid with a beret and round glasses | **Feature 2, Presentation practice** + **Q&A rehearsal** · the **deck reader** and **slide-check** agents | Live presentation / Q&A session; the clay **slide board** scene on `/presentations` |
| **The chat buddy** | Cat with headphones | **Feature 3, Daily conversation** · the **conversation partner** | Live conversation session; the clay **microphone** scene on `/conversation` |

**Note:** The characters have no names in the app except Mate. "The interviewer", "the presenter's partner" and "the chat buddy" are just labels for this script. If you give them names in the video, add them to the app too, so the video matches the product.

## Scene 1: Hook, my story (0:00–0:30)

**Screen:** You, filmed from behind or the side, rehearsing in front of a mirror (or talking to an empty room / your laptop with nothing on screen). Hold on the silence after you finish. On screen, small text: *"...so how did I do?"*
Then cut to you facing the camera.

**Mascot:** None yet. This scene is you alone; the empty room is the point.

**Say:**
> Whenever I had to prepare for a presentation or a job interview, I practised the same way: talking to myself, or talking in front of the mirror. And when I finished... nothing. No one told me if my answer made sense, if I sounded nervous, or what I should work on. I was practising, but I had no idea what to improve.

---

## Scene 2: It's not just me (0:30–0:55)

**Screen:** Two cards appear while you speak:
1. "64% of employers: poor English is why graduates fail interviews" (JobStreet Malaysia, 2025)
2. "53%: weak communication & presentation skills" (JobStreet × MEF, 2025)
Then the line: *"Practice without feedback is just repetition."*

**Mascot:** None. Hold the characters back for the reveal.

**Say:**
> And I'm not the only one. Many students and fresh graduates, especially those with anxiety or stage fright, practise alone because it feels safer. But employers say communication is exactly where graduates fall short. So I built an AI assistant where you can practise safely, see exactly where to improve, and keep getting better.

**Note:** Sources: TECHNICAL.md refs [1] and [2]. Put them on screen in small text. The anxiety point is your own observation, so keep it phrased as "many" or "especially". Don't attach a number unless you cite one.

## Scene 3: Introducing SpeechMate and Mate (0:55–1:15)

**Screen:** SpeechMate home page. Highlight the three function cards one by one as you name them.

**Mascot:** **Mate** wakes up in the corner and its eyes follow the cursor. As you name each feature, cut in its character for a beat: robot (interview) → droid (presentation) → cat (conversation).

**Say:**
> This is SpeechMate, an AI speaking coach built for Malaysian speakers, and this is Mate, my companion along the way. SpeechMate understands English, Bahasa Malaysia and the mix we actually speak, and it doesn't mark a Malaysian accent as a mistake. Each way to practise has its own AI partner: an interviewer, an audience for my presentations, and a chat buddy for daily conversation. And none of them interrupts me while I speak.

## Scene 4: Demo, it knows who you are (1:15–1:25)

**Screen:** The `/profile` page: study level, field, speaking level, goal, challenges, feedback style. Pause briefly on the **"How your coach uses this"** preview.

**Mascot:** **Mate** hops when you save the profile.

**Say:**
> First, I tell SpeechMate about myself: my field, my level, and what I find hard. Every partner uses this to pitch its questions and feedback at my level.

## Scene 5: Feature 1, Mock interview: questions for YOUR interview (1:25–2:15)

**Screen:**
1. `/interview`: fill in the form slowly, so the viewer can read each field: **position**, **company**, **level**, **interview type**, your **background**, the **job description**, and upload your **resume**.
2. Split screen for 3–4 seconds:
   - left, greyed out: a generic list ("Tell me about yourself." "What are your strengths?" "Where do you see yourself in five years?");
   - right, highlighted: SpeechMate's generated plan, with each question's *what it tests* visible.
3. Click start: the live session, with the AI interviewer asking question 1.
4. You give a deliberately short answer, and the interviewer asks one follow-up.

**Mascot:** **The interviewer** (robot, square glasses, bow tie). It starts in the clay **briefcase** scene at the top of `/interview`. In the live session it's "sleeping" until you press start, then switches to "working" as it asks question 1. Zoom in on it as it asks the follow-up.

**Say:**
> Feature one: the mock interview. Most interview practice gives you the same general questions everyone gets. Here, I first tell SpeechMate what I'm actually interviewing for: the position, the company, the level, the job description, and my resume.
>
> The AI reads all of it and prepares a question plan for *this* job and *this* candidate: questions about the skills the role needs and the experience on my resume, the ones I should really expect. I can preview them before I start, so the practice matches the real interview.
>
> Then my interviewer wakes up. It asks each question, waits until I've finished, and if my answer is too short, it follows up, just like a real interviewer.

**Note:** Use a real role you care about (e.g. one you're applying for). For the split screen, use the questions SpeechMate actually generated. Pick one that clearly comes from your resume or the job description and zoom in on it. Use your own resume or a clearly fictional one.

## Scene 6: Interview results (2:15–2:45)

**Screen:**
1. Results page: overall score, the two-sentence summary, and the **four pillars** (Voice & delivery · Language & clarity · Body language · Confidence & presence).
2. Per-answer feedback: relevance, STAR structure, specificity, and the **stronger answer**.
3. The **three drills** and the **"Practise this now"** button.

**Mascot:** **Mate** is back in the corner. It shows "working" while the analysis runs, then celebrates when the results are ready.

**Say:**
> After the interview, I get my results: my voice, language, body language and confidence, in four simple pillars. For every answer, it tells me whether I really answered the question and whether it was well structured, and it shows a stronger version built only from facts I actually said. Then it gives me three drills to work on next.

**Note:** These results must come from the session recorded in Scene 5, not the seeded demo data. If your session shows a **Gaze Tunneling** link (needs ≥ 100 s of speech), add one line: "It even noticed that I look away right when I start to stumble."

---

## Scene 7: Feature 2, Presentation practice: does your talk match your slides? (2:45–3:35)

**Screen:**
1. `/presentations`: upload your deck (`.pptx` or `.pdf`).
2. The insights page: **summary**, **main message**, **structure**, and **each slide's key point**. Scroll slowly through them.
3. Present on camera, moving through the slides yourself (show 5–10 seconds).
4. Per-slide results: **match 1–5**, **covered** (quoting your words), **left out**, **tip**, time on slide. Zoom in on one slide where something was left out.

**Mascot:** **The presenter's partner** (droid, beret, round glasses). It starts in the clay **slide board** scene at the top of `/presentations`. While the deck is being read, cut to **Mate** "working" in the corner. During your talk the droid stays quiet: it listens and doesn't interrupt.

**Say:**
> Feature two: presentation practice. First, I upload my slides. Before I say a word, the AI reads the whole deck and writes down what it understands: the main message, the structure, and the key point of every slide.
>
> Then I present, on camera, moving through my slides at my own pace. My audience just listens. Afterwards, SpeechMate compares what I said on each slide with what that slide actually means. It shows what I covered, what I left out, and how well my explanation matched.
>
> This matters because when you present in your second language, it's easy to know the content but say it in a way that changes the meaning. SpeechMate checks that your audience hears what your slides really say.

**Note:** To make the point land, present one slide slightly wrong on purpose (skip its key point, or explain it vaguely) so the results show a low match with a clear "left out".

## Scene 7b: Sub-feature, Q&A rehearsal (3:35–3:45)

**Screen:** Click **"Start Q&A rehearsal"**. The AI asks a question that clearly refers to one of your slides. You answer.

**Mascot:** **The presenter's partner** droid switches to "working" and becomes the questioner.

**Say:**
> And after the talk, my audience asks questions drawn from my own slides, with the same per-answer feedback as the interview.

---

## Scene 8: Feature 3, Daily conversation (3:45–3:55)

**Screen:** `/conversation`: pick a topic. A short exchange where you mix in Manglish ("this week I'm quite busy lah, but okay") and the transcript shows it correctly. Cut to the results' **"You said / Try saying"** language tips.

**Mascot:** **The chat buddy** (cat with headphones). It starts in the clay **microphone** scene at the top of `/conversation`, then chats with you in the live session.

**Say:**
> Feature three: daily conversation with my chat buddy. Pick a topic and just talk. It understands how we really speak, and afterwards it shows what I said next to a more natural way to say it.

---

## Scene 9: How agentic AI improves learning (3:55–4:25)

**Screen:** A loop diagram that builds step by step, with each step's character above it:

| Step | Agent | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Know the learner | Learner profile | Mate |
| Plan | Question planner (interview) · deck reader + Q&A planner (presentation) | Robot · Droid |
| Practise | Interviewer · audience · conversation partner | Robot · Droid · Cat |
| Check | Slide check · answer feedback with fact-check | Droid · Robot |
| Coach | Drills chosen only from measured issues | Mate |
| Practise again | "Practise this now" → Progress | Mate |

Then show Mate's chat opening and answering "What should I practise next?" with a link to `/progress`.

**Mascot:** All four characters together for the first time, arranged around the loop. **Mate** ends in the centre and opens its chat.

**Say:**
> Behind these characters is not one chatbot, but a team of AI agents, each with one job. One plans your questions from your job or your slides. One runs the interview and decides when to follow up. One reads your deck so it can check your talk. And a coach turns only what was actually measured into your next drills. It can't invent a problem.
>
> Mate, my companion, knows my profile and my recent sessions, and tells me what to practise next. So instead of one-off feedback, I get a learning loop: practise, get coached, practise again, and watch myself improve.

**Note:** Keep the claims to what the code does. Turn order is enforced by the server (the LLM only phrases reactions), stronger answers are fact-checked, and coaching only selects from measured issues. Saying "guardrailed agents" is accurate. Saying "fully autonomous" is not.

## Scene 10: Close (4:25–4:45)

**Screen:** Three stat tiles, then the `/progress` page, then the logo and tagline.
- "4.9% / 7.3% word error rate: real English / Malay speech"
- "Recordings deleted after analysis"
- "Every score explained: How it's measured"

**Mascot:** Mirror the opening shot. You in front of the mirror again, but now **Mate** is beside you. It spins ("Nice one!") on the last line.

**Say:**
> It hears Malaysian speakers accurately, my recordings are deleted after analysis, and every score is explained, never guessed.
>
> No more practising to the mirror. SpeechMate: practise, and finally know what to improve.

**Note:** The WER figures are from `docs/benchmarks/stt-groq-cloud-2026-10-15.md`. Manglish is 16.5% on *synthetic* speech. If you mention it, say "synthetic". Don't put user-testing or improvement numbers on screen until you've measured them.

## Before recording: checklist

- [ ] **Characters:** record each partner in both states ("sleeping" before start, "working" while speaking). Record Mate idle, hopping, working and with its chat open. Clay scenes only render on desktop width.
- [ ] **Scene 9 group shot:** take a clean screenshot of each character on a plain background, so you can arrange them in your editor.
- [ ] **Interview (Scene 5):** use a real role. Total ≥ 100 s of speech so Gaze Tunneling can run. Give one answer under 25 words to trigger the follow-up.
- [ ] **Interview split screen:** screenshot the generated question plan and pick the most tailored question.
- [ ] **Presentation (Scene 7):** present one slide slightly wrong on purpose so the "left out" result is visible.
- [ ] Use real results pages from your own account for every feature. No seeded demo data.
- [ ] Hide the browser bookmarks bar and notifications. Turn on light or dark theme and keep it the same throughout (the characters change with the theme).
- [ ] Screen recording at 1080p. Record the voiceover separately so you can re-take lines.
- [ ] Check the timing: read the full narration aloud with a timer before editing.
